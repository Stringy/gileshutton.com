I wrote this for the LettersLive competition this year, and unfortunately it didn't go anywhere. But I said I'd put it on this blog and so it goes on this blog. It's a bit raw and personal, but here we are.

The brief was to write a letter to your past, present, or future self. I chose the past.

---

Dear me,

I know this letter doesn't find you well, but trust me, lad, you're nearly there.

Those plans in your head are difficult to shift. I remember. They stick in your mind when you're walking home from work, when you're eating dinner, when you're talking with friends. And you're thinking that thirty is enough years for anyone. But it isn't. Thirty years is barely enough time to get started.

If you were a walnut tree, it is only after at least thirty years you'd be mature enough to turn into furniture. You are a little too skinny for anything substantial, but perhaps you'd make a nice hat stand.

If you were a cheese, after thirty years you'd develop the kind of odour that strips paint, and the kinds of complex flavours reserved only for those willing to eat on their own in a well-ventilated field.

And if you were a garden, with thirty years you could grow abundance in every corner. You could take over the lawn, fill the beds with colour, host birds and bugs and bees and life. You could sit with yourself on those warm summer evenings, lost within undergrowth known only to you.

Imagine what you could do with another five years. Another ten, even? To tell you the truth I have a good idea of what's coming, but I don't want to spoil the surprise. Let me instead tell you this: with the exception of our knees, our back, and our knowledge of youthful lingo, everything improves with age. Everything.

It's looking like forty years aren't enough either and the garden is slowly blooming. The summers shine and even the winters glisten, but it takes planning and thought and careful decisions and there are always more jobs to do. Weeding, pruning, mowing. Raking up the leaves and scrubbing the encroaching moss. Whether it is growing the tree, ageing the cheese, or tending the garden, there is a satisfaction in life that I know you'll find. If my memory is right, deep down you know all this already, but it is difficult to see through the fog. Just remember that you are strong, you are resilient, and you are doing your best. I'm evidence enough for that.

I've enclosed an envelope coated with first class stamps to cover the distance, and I want you to write back with all your worries and all your anger. Fill a page. Fill five. And then post it all to me. Or don't. The job's done as long as there are words on the page and not in your head. You'll see.

We've come so far, you and I, and we have so much more to do. It would be such an awful waste to stop now. We're going to write more letters, we're going to tend the garden, and we're going to keep going.

Remember: you've already given me all your best,

Giles
